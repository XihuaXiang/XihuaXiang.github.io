DockLoop

Active project

↑ Back to projects

Connect everything. Keep track of anything.

Originally developed for the 2026 Swinburne Student Hackathon, where our team won 1st place overall, DockLoop is a system designed to connect, organise and track belongings, from everyday essentials to workplace assets. At its core is a universal connection standard we're developing for individuals, families and teams.

I played a leading role in the initial ideation and product design, developed the entire interactive app demo, and helped shape the product roadmap. Using simulated data, the demo showcases planned functionality, allowing users to see and interact with changes, such as a key being removed from its home dock.

Since the hackathon, we've continued refining the physical connection standard and developing the smart layer to integrate real-world sensors and data.

FSAE suspension design

Past university project

↑ Back to projects

As part of a university Formula SAE project, our team was tasked with designing a suspension system capable of withstanding the demanding loads of competitive racing while maintaining on-track performance.

I took responsibility for the wheel hubs and rims, designing the components in SolidWorks and evaluating their structural performance through simulation and physical load testing. This involved assessing how the designs responded to expected loads and identifying potential weaknesses.

The project gave me practical experience in taking mechanical components from initial design through to validation, while contributing to a larger, interconnected engineering system.

University bridge project

Past university project

↑ Back to projects

Our team was challenged to design and construct a lightweight bridge capable of supporting the greatest possible load while minimising material use.

We developed a hybrid structure combining 3D-printed abutments, steel arches, tension cables and a wooden deck. I contributed to the initial research, physical assembly and load testing, gaining practical experience in how material selection and structural design influence performance.

The finished bridge weighed just 372 g yet supported a tested load of 3,100 N, achieving the highest strength-to-weight ratio in the project. Testing also revealed significant deflection under peak loading, highlighting the importance of balancing structural strength with stiffness.

NFC networking cards

Active personal project

↑ Back to projects

I started this project to make connecting with people at large events easier. Each networking card contains two independently programmable NFC chips, so users can choose what each chip links to.

The cards are 3D-printed in PLA, with the lettering produced using dual-nozzle 3D printing.

We are refining the appearance of the cards and the readability of the printed lettering.
